UNSEEN

The 2026 grand-prix car, opened up

UNSEEN is an interactive 3D explainer of a representative 2026 Formula 1™ car. It has six stations, each built around one control and one physical idea: change the input, watch the car and its live readouts respond, then read a short explanation of why.

Open the explainer

01 · Downforce

Drag the speed and watch downforce and drag grow with it. Compare the front wing, the floor and the rear wing, expose the floor in an exploded view, or flip the rig for a ceiling test that compares downforce with the car's weight.

02 · Active aero

Switch the wings between Corner Mode and Straight Mode and compare drag and downforce, with estimated power demand and top speed. The model follows the 2026 mode switch, which replaces DRS.

03 · Energy

Scrub through a simulated lap to follow battery charge, motor deployment, braking recovery and super clipping. X-ray the car to reveal the power unit and animate the energy paths.

04 · Braking

Play or scrub a braking zone and inspect deceleration, forward load transfer, suspension dive, glowing brake discs, regenerative harvest and heat.

05 · Tow

Change the gap to a car ahead and compare the leading and following cars' drag and downforce in an illustrative wake model, including the power saved and the grip given up.

06 · Aero map

The tool aerodynamicists use for a ground-effect car: downforce, drag and balance charted against front and rear ride height. Lower the car and watch the floor's suction grow until, run too low, the diffuser stalls and the car porpoises.

Where the numbers come from

Regulation facts cite the FIA 2026 Technical Regulations (Section C). Quantities teams don't publish, such as aero coefficients, grip and brake behaviour, are model assumptions and are labelled as estimates in the app. The tow wake is illustrative, not CFD. The aero map's shapes come from published moving-ground wind-tunnel tests, set into the 2026 floor geometry. The physics models are kept separate from the Three.js scene and covered by unit tests; the source is on GitHub.

The explainer needs a browser with WebGL 2. On a phone, hold it sideways.
